An engagement that ends

The goal is not for you to need us for long. An engagement runs in four stages, and the last one is handing you the keys.

the process

Four stages

Typical duration: a few weeks for an assessment on its own, up to a few months for a full setup with training.

01

We take stock

A one-hour conversation, free and with no commitment, to understand where you stand. Nothing to prepare, and no need to know the vocabulary.

02

We assess what you have

We look at what already exists and give you a readable picture: what works, what does not, what it costs, and the order to tackle it in.

03

We build

Setup, connections, compliance. You see progress at every stage, described in language you can repeat internally without translating it.

04

We hand you the keys

Training for your team and written documentation. At the end, the setup is yours and runs without us. That was the destination from the start.

the difference

Why not just use an agency?

Agencies are good at their job. It simply is not the same job — and on one specific point, their interests and yours do not line up.

An agency

Runs your campaigns

It plans, buys and reports back. That is useful, and plenty of brands are right to keep one. But the technical layer stays on their side: if you leave, you start from scratch. Their interest is in continuing, not in making you self-sufficient.

Passerelle Consulting

Makes you self-sufficient, then leaves

We are paid to install a capability inside your company: the tools are in your name, the logins are yours, your team is trained. We take no commission on your ad spend, so we have no interest in you spending more.

frequently asked

What we get asked most

Do we have to fire our agency to work with you?
No, and it is rarely a good idea. Many of our clients keep their agency for creative and day-to-day management, and bring only the technical and data side in-house. The two work well together once the roles are clear.
Are we too small for this?
It is not about company size but about advertising budget. Below a certain annual spend, running this in-house costs more than it returns — and we will say so plainly rather than sell you an engagement. One conversation is usually enough to settle it.
Nobody here is technical. Is that a problem?
No. It is the most common situation, and precisely why training is part of the engagement. We expect no technical vocabulary from you: translating is our job, not your job to guess.
What does it cost?
It depends on the scope, and we prefer to quote after the assessment rather than before. We bill a fixed fee per stage, agreed in advance — never a percentage of your ad spend. The first conversation is free.

The first step is just a conversation

One hour, free, no commitment. If this is not our territory we will say so — and point you elsewhere where we can.
